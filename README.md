# pet friendly hotels las vegas: real dog fees, the properties that waive them, and how to book without leaving cash on the table

Bringing a dog to Las Vegas is not the hard part. Paying for it is. The city has hundreds of hotels that call themselves pet friendly, and the price of that friendliness ranges from $0 to $200 per night per dog, with resort fees stacked on top and a refundable deposit sometimes sitting on your card for a week after checkout. Two hotels on the same stretch of Paradise Road can differ by $150 a night for a dog of identical size.

## The three numbers that decide your pet budget

Every pet-friendly hotel in Las Vegas is really three separate charges. Read them before you compare anything.

**The nightly pet fee.** Either per pet or per stay, taxed or not. Vdara's official policy charges $150 per night per dog in standard rooms and $200 per night per dog in suites. Motel 6's Tropicana property is the opposite extreme, charging nothing at all.

**The deposit.** Flamingo takes a $100 per-night refundable deposit on top of its $100 nightly pet charge; Golden Nugget adds a $100 per-pet refundable deposit on top of $80 per pet, per night. Deposits come back, but they tie up your card.

**The resort fee.** Nothing to do with your dog and usually the biggest sneaky line item. Golden Nugget's is $32 plus 13% tax per night. Strip resorts commonly sit higher. This matters twice over: it isn't part of the cashback base on most travel bookings, and it doesn't shrink because your room has a water bowl in it.

Weight rules are the other trap. A 50-pound cap covers Flamingo, Golden Nugget and Residence Inn Convention Center. Vdara allows up to two dogs with a combined weight under 100 pounds. The Cosmopolitan, per Time Out's 2026 guide to Vegas pet stays, applies no weight limit at all.

## Where the "no pet fee" claim falls apart

Vegas blogs repeat a short list of no-fee hotels, and the list is unstable. Virgin Hotels gets described as charging nothing in one guide, a $75–90 nightly fee in another, and $175 per night on OTA property listings. That's not a rounding error, it's three different policies or three different vintages of the same policy. Virgin does run "pets stay free" promotions periodically, which is probably how the confusion started.

Motel 6 is the reliable zero-fee option — the "pets stay free" policy is a brand-wide rule, and the Tropicana property is a short walk from the MGM Grand end of the Strip. Rooms are plain. Nobody is pretending otherwise. If you're doing two nights and don't plan to spend daylight hours in the room, the math is hard to argue with.

La Quinta properties show up on no-fee lists too, but the details (weight caps, whether the fee is waived per property) come from third-party blogs rather than property pages. Call before you count on it.

One more thing worth knowing: a no-fee hotel is not automatically the cheapest stay. If the zero-fee property is 20 minutes off-Strip, you're paying for rideshares both ways, and the $150 Strip property may end up within $20 of it. Run the whole trip, not the pet line.

## What actually matters for the dog

Fees aside, the difference between a good and bad pet stay in Vegas is walking distance and noise.

Vdara's setup is the most practical on the Strip: an all-suite, non-smoking, non-gaming building with a designated dog park on property. No casino floor to cross at 6am with a leash in one hand and a coffee in the other. Dogs must be crated or in a carrier if left alone, are limited to rooms, suites and the dog park, and cats aren't permitted.

The Cosmopolitan sits at the other end of the personality spectrum — high energy, center Strip, rooms with terraces, and per Time Out, two dog runs including a fenced area near the Chelsea Pool, with no weight limit. If your dog is bombproof, it's a great location. If your dog startles at crowds, the terrace won't save you.

Downtown, Golden Nugget puts dogs close to the Fremont Street action with a relief area outside the Rush Tower valet, but the rules are tighter: 50 pounds combined for two dogs, no cats, and your dog can't be left in the room unattended.

Flamingo deserves a specific warning. Pet rooms can't be booked online — you have to call (702) 733-3111 — and 2026 guest reviews on BringFido describe the pet floor as far from the relief area, with worn carpets and inconsistent amenities. The fee is $100 a night plus a $100 nightly deposit. At that price, other options deserve a look first.

For extended stays, Residence Inn properties make more sense than a casino resort: kitchens, laundry, pet parks, and a pet policy that rewards longer trips. The Convention Center location charges $20 a day capped with a $100 one-time cleaning fee, which gets cheaper the longer you stay. The Henderson/Green Valley location charges a flat $150 per room per stay and accepts cats.

## Questions that come up before booking

**Do any Las Vegas hotels allow dogs over 50 pounds?** Yes. Vdara allows two dogs with a combined weight under 100 pounds. Westgate allows a 60-pound dog, or two dogs totalling 60 pounds. Otonomus accepts dogs up to about 45 kg each.

**Can I leave my dog in the room while I'm at a show?** At Vdara only if it's crated or in a carrier. Golden Nugget doesn't permit unattended dogs at all. Assume the stricter rule unless the hotel tells you otherwise in writing.

**Does cashback cover the pet fee?** Generally no. Pet charges are fees, and fees typically sit outside the cashback calculation along with taxes and resort fees. Some properties collect the pet charge at check-in rather than through the booking site, which settles the question.

**Is the cheapest pet-friendly hotel actually the cheapest trip?** Rarely, once rideshares and resort fees are added. A $0 pet fee off-Strip against a $100 pet fee within walking distance of everything usually comes down to $30 or $40 either way — at which point pick the better location.

**When should I book?** At least a week out for better rates on Vegas pet-friendly properties, and check whether your dates fall on a convention week, when Strip rates can double.
